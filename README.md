# CS-255

  The client, DriverPass, wanted a new piece of software to help student drivers pass their DMV test.  The client wanted a web-based, platform-agnostic application.  The attached business requirements and systems design documents were created after examination of the recorded interview between the development team and the clients.

  One thing that was done especially well were the diagrams.  These were created directly from the features the client stated in the interview alongside the supporting features.  However, the diagrams often present a detailed overview, and as a result, can become quite busy and hard to read.  Therefore, one area of improvement would be to take a more high-level approach in the diagraming process.

  When interpreting the client's needs, the approach taken was to consider the client's requests to be the high-level, broad strokes description of the requirement.  From there, the supporting details and requirements were filled in.  Certain aspects, such as security features, were not explicitly mentioned or described in strong detail by the client.  In these areas, the approach taken was to design the system with maximal security in mind, especially as sensitive information such as financial and Social Security identification were likely to be present.

  As the saying goes, "You don't become an engineer by adding material, you become an engineer by removing material until there's nothing left able to be removed."    This saying typified the software design approach, with systems being removed or combined over multiple iterations until removing components actively interfered with the program's operation.
